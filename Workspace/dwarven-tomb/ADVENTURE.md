# The Rootbound Oath

**Adventure Type:** One-shot dungeon mission  
**Game System:** D&D 5e (2024), SRD 5.2.1  
**Recommended Character Level:** 5  
**Recommended Party Size:** 4 characters  
**Expected Play Time:** Approximately 4 hours  
**Primary Location:** The sealed tomb of Vhaldrik Stone-Sworn, beneath a wooded cliff  
**Tone:** Mournful, subterranean, tense, and ultimately heroic  
**Primary Themes:** Oaths, stewardship, the cost of disturbing the dead, roots reclaiming stone, and redemption

---

# 1. ADVENTURE AT A GLANCE

## Premise

A root-choked opening has appeared in a cliff above the hamlet of Brackenford, and grey water is flowing from it into the village spring. The opening leads to the dwarven tomb of Vhaldrik Stone-Sworn, whose buried oath-engine once kept an ancient death-spirit sealed beneath the mountain. A grave-robber has stolen the engine's regulating shard and is using the tomb's own roots and dead to force the seal open.

The characters must enter the compact tomb, learn what the dwarves actually sealed, recover or replace the missing shard, and decide whether the tomb's last living keeper deserves rescue—or judgment.

## Primary Objective

Prevent the Hollow Oath from fully awakening by restoring or destroying the oath-engine in the central chamber before the tomb's dead and roots overrun Brackenford.

## Secondary Objectives

- Recover the regulating shard from the grave-robber or the corrupted reliquary.
- Learn the truth of Vhaldrik's oath and give the dead a proper accounting.
- Rescue Keeper Dagna Flintvein, if she still lives, or preserve her testimony.

## Stakes

**If the characters succeed:**  
The grey seepage stops, the dead return to rest, and Brackenford's spring becomes safe after a day of flushing and cleansing.

**If the characters partially succeed:**  
The awakening is contained but the tomb collapses around the oath-engine, or the engine is destroyed at the cost of permanently poisoning the spring and leaving a lesser haunting behind.

**If the characters fail:**  
The Hollow Oath escapes into the root network. Brackenford becomes the first of several settlements where the dead rise whenever the ground is disturbed.

## Adventure Shape

Briefing at Brackenford -> root-choked entrance and west antechamber -> investigation of the central tomb junction -> exploration of the north reliquary and east accounting room -> optional south crypt -> confrontation at the oath-engine -> sealing, destruction, or release.

## Expected Challenge Mix

| Challenge Type | Approximate Share | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Combat | 35% | Two short fights or one fight plus a challenging finale; enemies use the tomb and retreat intelligently. |
| Social | 15% | Questioning Keeper Dagna and bargaining with the bound spirit. |
| Exploration | 20% | Reading the compact map, examining the four tomb zones, and tracking the root intrusion. |
| Skills / Problem Solving | 20% | Interpreting dwarven funerary marks, aligning the oath-engine, and finding fail-forward clues. |
| Hazards / Resource Pressure | 10% | Root constriction, unstable stone, rising necrotic seepage, and a three-stage timer. |

# 2. SOURCE IMAGE INTERPRETATION

## Visible Elements

- A broad ochre exterior area on the left, divided from the dark interior by a rough cliff face.
- One irregular, root-filled opening in the cliff leading into the tomb; there is no second visible entrance.
- A sandy or dusty west antechamber with scattered debris and a large upright stone feature.
- A central rectangular stone chamber with a narrow north corridor, a narrow south corridor, and side connections west and east.
- A round chamber at the north end containing a bright gold or fire-colored object.
- A narrow east passage leading to a small rectangular room with a visible chest, slab, or cabinet-like object.
- A larger south room containing a long central stone feature and several round floor or lid details.
- Dark, regular flagstone flooring and heavy outlined masonry throughout the interior.

## Implied Elements

- **Likely history:** This is a deliberately planned dwarven funerary complex rather than a natural cave. The central room distributes access to a reliquary, an accounting room, and a communal crypt.
- **Current occupants or factions:** A living tomb keeper, a grave-robber, disturbed dead, and invasive roots altered by necromantic energy.
- **Potential threat:** The gold-colored object is a ward or power source whose removal destabilizes the tomb; the roots are carrying the disturbance outward.
- **Potential mystery:** Why would a tomb need an oath-engine, and what exactly did Vhaldrik swear to contain?
- **Potential reward or discovery:** Dwarven oath-metal, ancestral records, and a relic that can protect a community if handled responsibly.
- **Mood:** Quiet, geometric, solemn, and claustrophobic, with warm gold at the heart of an otherwise cold and dark place.

## Adventure Seed Derived From the Image

The map's single root-choked mouth and branching tomb plan suggest a sealed memorial whose protective system is being breached from the outside in. The bright round reliquary becomes the visible heart of that system, while the east room preserves its rules and the south crypt supplies the personal cost of the oath. The adventure therefore treats every branch as a different way to understand or influence the same failing ward rather than as disconnected rooms.

## Image Elements Used in the Adventure

| Image Element | Adventure Use |

| Dusty west antechamber and upright stone | Arrival scene, first clue, and a defensible place where the characters can meet Keeper Dagna. |
| Central junction | The tomb's oath-engine control chamber and the point from which sound, light, and danger spread. |

# 3. GM SYNOPSIS

The tomb of Vhaldrik Stone-Sworn was built around a dwarven oath-engine: a relic that converts the sworn names of the dead into a binding seal. Vhaldrik, founder of the nearby Stone-Sworn hold, used it to imprison the Hollow Oath, a psychic residue formed from a massacre in which he broke a promise to surrender safely. The spirit is not simply a monster; it is the accusation of the betrayed dead, given shape by generations of guilt and the engine's magic.

Keeper Dagna Flintvein has maintained the tomb for thirty years. She recently discovered that the western roots had entered the antechamber and began carrying grey water from the seal. She sent for help, but the opportunistic relic-hunter Sella Varn arrived first. Sella stole the amber oath-shard from the round north reliquary, believing it to be a valuable gemstone. The theft woke the tomb's guardian dead and trapped Dagna in the south crypt.

Sella is now in the central chamber, trying to force the shard into the old control socket. She does not understand that the shard is a regulator, not a key. If she succeeds, the Hollow Oath will manifest through the roots and use the shard to leave the tomb. She can still be reasoned with if the characters expose the records in the east room or offer a way to escape with a lesser relic. If attacked, she fights as a desperate occult thief rather than a loyal cultist.

The characters can restore the engine by returning the shard and speaking the names recorded in the east room, destroy the shard and collapse the seal with a costly blast, or bargain with the Hollow Oath. The last option can end the immediate threat if the characters acknowledge Vhaldrik's betrayal and promise a public memorial, but it leaves the spirit as a demanding guardian rather than destroying it. The map's branches provide the information and tools for these choices; no single room or check is mandatory.

# 4. ADVENTURE BACKGROUND

## Historical Background

Vhaldrik Stone-Sworn led a group of miners and refugees through a mountain war. At the Battle of Black Sill, he promised the enemy's surrendered workers safe passage, then ordered the tunnel sealed when pursuit arrived. The trapped workers died. Vhaldrik built the tomb to preserve their names and bound the psychic remnant of their anger beneath an oath-engine powered by the amber shard, a piece of magically vitrified heartwood from the first tree growing over the mass grave.

The tomb's four visible zones each served a funerary purpose. The west antechamber received mourners from the cliff path. The central chamber held the engine's balancing mechanism. The round north reliquary housed the amber shard. The east room kept the names, terms, and maintenance records. The south crypt held Vhaldrik and the honored dead of his hold in shared rest.

## Recent Events

Three nights ago, Sella Varn followed old smuggling marks to the cliff. She pried open the root-filled entrance, entered the tomb, and removed the amber shard. The tomb's protective light went out, grey seepage reached the spring, and the dead began knocking from below the south crypt's lids. Dagna confronted Sella and was driven into the crypt when the central guardian awakened.

Sella has since tried to install the shard in the central socket. Each attempt has made the roots thicken and the stone shake. She has one intact healing draught, a satchel of chisels, and a copied fragment of the east-room records.

## Current Situation

When play begins, the characters are at the root-choked entrance. Dagna is alive but trapped behind the southern crypt's disturbed dead, unless the characters take more than roughly two hours in-world. Sella is in or near the central chamber. The amber shard is either in Sella's possession or lodged in the wrong socket, depending on how quickly the characters advance. The Hollow Oath can whisper through any root, but it cannot yet leave the tomb.

Use a three-step **Tomb Pulse** clock. Advance it whenever the characters make a loud, prolonged failure, take a short rest inside, or spend about 30 minutes debating without acting. At Pulse 1, grey water pools in the west antechamber. At Pulse 2, the central chamber's doors and roots begin shifting; undead in the south crypt awaken. At Pulse 3, the Hollow Oath manifests in the finale with its strongest ability and the spring begins to rise in a poisonous flood.

## What Happens Without the Characters

After the next Tomb Pulse, Sella fits the shard into the central socket and accidentally opens a hairline breach. The Hollow Oath animates the roots and attacks her, but she survives long enough to flee through the entrance with the shard if not stopped. At the following Pulse, the bound spirit reaches Brackenford's spring. Each later hour allows it to animate more dead and spread the grey water to the road. The GM should keep this progression visible through tremors, distant knocking, and increasingly warm gold light from the north chamber.

# 5. LOCATION OVERVIEW

## Where Is This?

The tomb lies inside a low wooded cliff half a mile above Brackenford. The visible exterior side of the map is a bare ochre shelf or path outside the cliff; the dungeon occupies the dark stone cut into its eastern face. The characters approach from the open shelf and enter through the only visible breach, then move through the west antechamber to the central junction and its north, east, and south branches.

## Environment

- **Terrain:** Worked dwarven flagstone and dressed masonry inside; dusty, root-laced rubble at the entrance and in the west antechamber.
- **Climate / Weather:** Cool and dry underground, with damp grey seepage increasing as the oath-engine fails. The exterior is breezy and overcast unless the GM prefers another local condition.
- **Visibility / Lighting:** The interior is dark. The amber shard gives a weak gold glow in the north reliquary and, after Pulse 1, faint pulses through cracks and roots. Characters without darkvision need light.
- **Travel Conditions:** The entrance rubble is uneven but passable. The narrow branch corridors allow single-file movement. The south room has enough space for a four-person party to maneuver around the central slab, but not to ignore the crypt lids.
- **Local Dangers:** Unstable roots, disturbed burial lids, necrotic seepage, and the possibility that noise carries through the central junction.
- **Important Local Rules:** A spoken true name or oath suppresses the Hollow Oath's whisper for one round in its presence. The amber shard is warm, not hot; it responds to honest testimony and dims when used to support a lie.

## Key Landmarks

1. **The Root-Mouth** — The only entrance and the visible point where the tomb's corruption has broken into the living world.
2. **The Four-Way Oath Junction** — The central chamber, where every branch of the tomb converges and where the final sealing must occur.
3. **The Gold Reliquary** — The round north chamber containing the amber regulating shard and the tomb's most obvious source of power.
4. **The Stone-Sworn Crypt** — The larger south chamber, where named dead and the trapped keeper reveal the personal cost of the old oath.

# 6. IMPORTANT NPCS AND FACTIONS

## Keeper Dagna Flintvein

**Role:** Last appointed keeper of the tomb and living witness to its history  
**Goal:** Restore the oath-engine if possible, but prevent the Hollow Oath from being used to punish innocent people  
**Motivation:** Dagna has maintained the tomb because her family helped build it; she believes stewardship means telling the truth about Vhaldrik's crime  
**Attitude Toward the Characters:** Grateful but wary; she assumes armed strangers may be more interested in relics than in the dead  
**What She Knows:** The amber object regulates the engine; the east records contain the names and the exact terms of the binding; the south crypt has a maintenance lever beneath the long slab  
**What She Hides:** She concealed the worst line of the record from Brackenford's leaders, fearing that public knowledge would provoke grave robbing and anti-dwarven violence  
**Portrayal:** A compact, soot-streaked dwarf with a cracked brass key-ring; she speaks in careful inventories until emotion breaks through  

### If Negotiated With

Dagna gives the characters the key-ring, tells them the truth, and helps with one engine check if they promise not to take the shard as personal treasure. If they admit that destruction is the only safe option, she accepts it, provided the names are preserved first. She becomes fully cooperative if the characters free her from the south crypt or show her the east-room account.

### If Threatened or Attacked

Dagna retreats behind the south crypt's central slab and uses the key-ring to jam a burial lid open as cover. She will not fight the characters unless cornered, but she loudly names Vhaldrik's betrayal so the Hollow Oath can hear it. If killed, her key-ring and last testimony remain available, but the party loses her help in the finale.

## Sella Varn

**Role:** Grave-robber and accidental antagonist  
**Goal:** Escape with a valuable relic and enough information to sell it to a collector  
**Motivation:** Sella grew up in debt and has convinced herself that dead empires cannot be robbed; she did not expect the tomb to contain a conscious grievance  
**Attitude Toward the Characters:** Suspicious, quick-witted, and ready to bargain; she prefers a clean escape to a fight  
**What She Knows:** The shard is warm, whispers names, and does not fit the socket she found; Dagna is alive in the south room; the tomb is becoming more dangerous  
**What She Hides:** She copied part of the east-room record and knows the word “surrender,” but has not admitted that her theft caused the breach  
**Portrayal:** Dusty travelling leathers, one expensive glove, and a smile that appears a moment before every lie  

### If Negotiated With

Sella can hand over the shard, lead the characters to the records room, or help distract the Hollow Oath in exchange for safe passage and permission to keep a nonmagical funerary token. A DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion or Deception) check, a promise of legal protection, or proof that the shard is not a saleable gemstone moves her. If shown the full record, she offers a sincere apology but still tries to leave.

### If Threatened or Attacked

Sella uses the narrow corridors, throws dust to obscure sight, and retreats toward the central junction. She surrenders at 10 hit points or fewer if promised that she will not be abandoned in the tomb. If she escapes, she leaves behind her copied record, which still provides the essential clue.

## The Hollow Oath

**Role:** Bound psychic remnant and final adversary  
**Goal:** Be acknowledged, prevent the oath-engine from disguising the betrayal, and either obtain a memorial or escape into the root network  
**Motivation:** It is made from the anger and last thoughts of workers who were promised safety and sealed underground  
**Attitude Toward the Characters:** Testing and accusatory rather than immediately murderous; it reacts to lies and sincere acknowledgment differently  
**What It Knows:** Every spoken name in the tomb, the truth of Vhaldrik's order, and the engine's three possible fates  
**What It Hides:** It does not want indiscriminate revenge. If properly acknowledged, it can release its hold and become a boundary-keeper rather than a roaming undead thing  
**Portrayal:** A voice from several throats, heard through roots and stone; use singular grammar until the characters learn that many dead speak through it  

### If Negotiated With

The Hollow Oath accepts three specific acts: naming the betrayal, acknowledging the harm without excuses, and promising a memorial or restitution. It rejects offers of gold, threats to “put the dead back,” and claims that Vhaldrik acted for the greater good. Use the finale's three-success bargain track.

### If Threatened or Attacked

It manifests fully and uses the central junction as a battlefield. It attacks the loudest or most dishonest speaker first, but it never pursues beyond the tomb while the amber shard remains inside. At half its hit points it offers the characters one last chance to speak truth; this is an invitation, not a stun or forced pause.

## Brackenford Spring-Keepers

**Role:** Local faction and source of the mission  
**Goal:** Stop the grey water and protect the hamlet's only reliable spring  
**Motivation:** The seepage has already sickened livestock and threatens the settlement's winter stores  
**Attitude Toward the Characters:** Urgent, practical, and willing to pay in supplies rather than coin  
**What They Know:** The cliff opening appeared three nights ago; Dagna used to visit the spring and warned people not to disturb the old stones  
**What They Hide:** The spring-keepers knew the tomb was a dwarven burial place but kept quiet because the cliff sheltered them from raiders  
**Portrayal:** Muddy boots, clipped sentences, and constant glances toward the water barrel  

### If Negotiated With

They provide a handcart, six hooded lanterns, rope, and a signed promise of 150 gp from the hamlet's winter fund. They also agree to build a memorial if the characters bring back the names.

### If Threatened or Attacked

They flee and spread the story that the characters are tomb robbers. This does not stop the adventure, but full payment and local goodwill are lost.

# 7. ADVENTURE SUMMARY

## Part 1 — The Root-Mouth

The characters reach the only entrance through the cliff and must pass roots that have grown through the tomb's masonry. In the dusty west antechamber they find the first evidence that someone entered recently, and they may encounter Sella or hear Dagna's calls carried through the stone. The first Tomb Pulse should be visible but not yet catastrophic.

## Part 2 — The Broken Accounting

At the central junction, the characters can investigate the north reliquary and east records room in either order. They learn that the amber shard regulates rather than unlocks the engine, discover the names of the betrayed workers, and determine that the final work must happen at the junction. Sella can be confronted, recruited, or allowed to flee.

## Part 3 — The Names Below

The south crypt contains Dagna, disturbed burial lids, and the maintenance lever that makes restoration safer. This part gives the characters a chance to rescue an ally, recover the complete testimony, and decide whether they will restore the engine, destroy it, or bargain with the dead. It is optional if time is short, but skipping it makes the finale more dangerous and less informed.

## Optional Encounter — The Unquiet Ledger

If the party searches the east room carefully or returns there after meeting Dagna, the names in the ledger appear as fresh charcoal marks. Reading them aloud grants a benefit in the finale but also draws the Hollow Oath's attention. This is a short roleplaying and puzzle scene that can replace a combat encounter for a group that prefers investigation.

## Finale — The Oath at the Junction

The Hollow Oath manifests at the central engine. The characters must survive its pressure while completing one of three objective tracks: restore the engine with the shard and names, destroy the system at a cost, or bargain through honest acknowledgment. Earlier discoveries, Sella's fate, Dagna's assistance, and the state of the Tomb Pulse all materially change the scene.

## Required Beats

- The characters learn that the tomb's disturbance is connected to the amber shard and the root network.
- They obtain enough information to choose a finale route, even if one room or NPC is bypassed.
- The Hollow Oath reaches the central junction and the characters resolve its breach.

## Optional Beats

- Confronting Sella in the north reliquary or persuading her to help.
- Rescuing Dagna from the south crypt.
- Reading the ledger's names aloud before the finale.
- Discovering the full moral history of Vhaldrik rather than treating the bound spirit as a simple monster.

## Skip / Recovery Paths

If the characters bypass Sella, she reaches the junction and becomes a frightened witness during the finale. If they never find the east records, Dagna can provide the three necessary names from memory, but restoration checks are made with disadvantage on the first attempt only. If Dagna dies or is left behind, her key-ring can be found on the south slab and the ledger still explains the engine. If the characters take the shard and try to leave, the Hollow Oath manifests at the root-mouth, forcing the same three finale choices in a smaller battlefield. If they destroy the shard early, use the destruction track immediately and treat the final scene as a controlled collapse rather than a search for a missing component.

No single failed check blocks the adventure. A missed clue costs time, advances the Tomb Pulse, or makes a later route more expensive.

# 9. GETTING STARTED

## Opening Situation

The characters are in Brackenford when a spring-keeper asks them to inspect the new opening in the cliff. Grey water has turned two goats sluggish, and a child who drank from the spring woke speaking in a voice that was not hers. The spring-keepers cannot spare enough people to investigate and know that Dagna Flintvein, the old tomb keeper, has not returned from the cliff.

## Adventure Hook

The hamlet offers 150 gp in pooled funds, free lodging, and a winter supply of healing herbs for stopping the contamination. Characters with dwarven, archaeological, religious, or local ties may also be told that the cliff contains the sealed tomb of Vhaldrik Stone-Sworn, a figure whose name is omitted from most local histories.

## Opening / Briefing Text

> The spring water in Brackenford runs clear until it reaches the bottom of the bucket. There it clouds to the colour of old ash. Above the hamlet, a raw gap splits the wooded cliff, and pale roots hang from it like wet fingers. A brass key-ring lies on the table between you, still marked with a smear of grey mud. “Dagna went in before dawn,” says the spring-keeper. “If she is alive, bring her home. If she is not, find out what woke the stones.”

## Mission Parameters

The characters are asked or motivated to:

1. Enter the root-choked cliff opening and determine the cause of the grey seepage.
2. Rescue Dagna if possible and recover the names or records needed to stabilize the tomb.
3. Stop the disturbance before it reaches Brackenford's spring; do not remove a relic for personal sale.

## What the Characters Know

- The opening in the cliff is the only known way into the tomb.
- Dagna has maintained the site for decades and would not normally leave it unattended.
- The grey water began after the opening appeared.
- Vhaldrik Stone-Sworn was a dwarven founder, but local histories avoid discussing the end of his rule.
- The tomb is likely dangerous because of unstable roots and disturbed burials, not because the spring-keepers have seen an army of monsters.

## What They Can Learn Before Leaving

| Topic | Method | Difficulty | Information |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Dagna's route | Question the spring-keepers or inspect the mud | DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Investigation) | Dagna entered alone carrying her brass key-ring; a second set of boot prints followed shortly afterward. |
| Vhaldrik's reputation | Ask elders, use relevant History, or examine a local monument | DC 13 Intelligence (History) | Vhaldrik was praised as a founder but blamed in one defaced inscription for sealing a tunnel after a surrender. |
| Safe approach | Inspect roots and cliff face | DC 13 Wisdom (Nature or Survival) | Cutting every root will cause a collapse; loosen the thick central roots and leave the living outer mat intact. |
| The shard | Ask about the warm light seen through the opening | DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) | The gold light is likely a ward or regulator, not ordinary treasure. |

A failed check still provides the information with one cost: the characters begin with one root-related complication in Part 1, such as a snagged pack, a broken lantern, or one lost hour.

## Likely Questions

### Can we bring reinforcements?

The spring-keepers can lend two commoners, but those helpers will wait outside with the cart and cannot safely enter the crypt. The tomb is too narrow for a larger rescue party.

### Is Dagna trustworthy?

She has kept the tomb closed for thirty years and warned the hamlet about the old stones. The spring-keepers have no reason to think she caused the breach.

### What happens if we take the relic?

The spring-keepers ask the characters to return with the truth and the names, not a trophy. The amber light has already caused sickness since it was disturbed.

### Do we have to fight the dead?

No. The tomb responds to names, honest testimony, and careful handling as well as to force. The spring-keepers do not know this, but Dagna's records do.

## Equipment / Preparation

The spring-keepers provide six hooded lanterns, 50 feet of rope, a handcart, chalk, a pry bar, and three bundles of bitterroot. Burning a bundle as an action creates a 10-foot-radius cloud for 1 minute that gives advantage on the next check made to resist or navigate the grey roots; a bundle is consumed. Characters can purchase or use normal adventuring equipment as available.

## Transition Into the Adventure

The characters follow the muddy path up through the trees. At the cliff shelf they see the root-mouth on the left side of the map and hear a slow metallic knocking from somewhere beyond the dusty chamber. The first character to step inside notices that the roots pulse once in time with the distant knocking, beginning Part 1.

# 10. PART 1 — THE ROOT-MOUTH

## Purpose

Part 1 establishes the tomb's physical logic, introduces the root corruption, gives the characters their first noncombat problem, and supplies enough evidence to locate the active disturbance. It should take 30–45 minutes.

## Situation

The root-choked opening is passable but unstable. The outer roots are living plants, while the thick inner roots have absorbed grey necrotic seepage from the tomb. Someone forced a path through recently. Dagna's brass key-ring is not here, but a snapped loop from it lies in the dust. Sella's boot prints lead toward the central junction.

## Scene Introduction

> The cliff's wound is wider than it looked from below. Ochre dust gives way to black flagstones beneath a mat of roots, and the air changes from wet forest to old iron. Beyond the roots, a low chamber opens around an upright slab streaked with handprints and powdered stone. Somewhere deeper, metal knocks against metal: three slow blows, then silence.

## Player Objective

Enter without causing a collapse, search the west antechamber, and determine whether Dagna or the intruder left a usable trail.

## Key Features

- **Root-mouth:** The only visible entrance; the outer roots can be moved, but hacking through the entire mat risks bringing down the cliff face.
- **Dusty antechamber:** A roughly rectangular west room with loose debris, scraped flagstones, and the upright stone feature shown on the map. It is large enough for the whole party but offers little cover.
- **Upright oath-marker:** The stone bears a worn dwarven handprint and a line of text: “A sealed word is still a word.” It is the first indication that this is an oath-site, not merely a burial place.
- **Central opening:** The antechamber narrows into the central junction through the map's visible stone connection. Sound carries clearly from there to all three branches.

## Approaches

### Combat

There is no required combat in this part. If the characters violently cut the roots, use the Root-Mouth hazard and have two Root-Scrabblers emerge from the disturbed tangle. They attack until one is destroyed, then retreat into cracks. This is an optional easy fight, not an ambush that should consume the session.

### Social

If the characters call for Dagna, she answers faintly from the direction of the south branch: “Names first. Then the light.” She cannot hear complex questions yet. If they call for the intruder, Sella calls back from beyond the central junction that she is “trying to fix what she broke,” then moves away unless they offer terms.

### Skill / Problem Solving

The characters can loosen the roots with the provided pry bar, bitterroot smoke, careful cutting, water, or magic. The upright marker can be read with dwarven knowledge, a rubbing, or patient cleaning. The trail can be followed without a check if the characters describe examining the dust; a check reveals useful details but is not a gate.

### Stealth / Avoidance

A quiet group can enter without advancing the Tomb Pulse. Characters who extinguish light and move one at a time have advantage on the check to avoid dislodging rubble, but they risk splitting the party in the narrow connection. Sella notices any loud approach from the central junction and prepares to flee.

### Creative Solutions

Reward solutions that preserve the living outer roots, support the cliff, or use the marker as a focus for truthful testimony. A character who speaks an honest reason for entering gains advantage on the next root-navigation check; the tomb responds to intent even before the characters understand why.

## Checks and Challenges

| Task | Skill / Method | Difficulty | Success | Failure / Complication |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Make a safe passage | DC 13 Strength (Athletics), Dexterity (Acrobatics), Wisdom (Nature or Survival), or suitable magic | 13 | The group enters without damage and keeps the Tomb Pulse unchanged | One creature takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage and falls prone; loud failure advances the Pulse by 1 |
| Read the oath-marker | DC 12 Intelligence (History or Investigation), Wisdom (Religion), or dwarven proficiency | 12 | Learn that the tomb's wards respond to spoken promises and that a sealed word matters | The meaning is still found later in the east records, but the characters hear a whisper and lose 10 minutes |
| Follow the recent trail | DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Survival) | 12 | Learn that one person entered after Dagna and carried a heavy object from the north branch toward the centre | The trail is found after searching the central junction, costing time but not blocking progress |
| Keep the cliff stable | DC 13 Strength with the pry bar, Dexterity with tools, or appropriate spell | 13 | The entrance remains usable for retreat | Falling grit and roots give disadvantage on the next check made in the west antechamber |

## Creatures / NPCs

- Keeper Dagna is alive but too distant to reach from this room.
- Sella Varn is somewhere beyond the central junction; she does not begin combat here.
- Two Root-Scrabblers appear only if the party makes the optional violent approach; use the Appendix A stat block.

## Tactics / Behaviour

**Before conflict:**  
The roots constrict around tools and ankles, not throats. They react to force and necrotic seepage, not to ordinary speech. Sella listens for a fight and tries to avoid being trapped between the party and the central room.

**During conflict:**  
Root-Scrabblers cling to stone and attack a character carrying a light or the pry bar. They do not pursue beyond the antechamber.

**Morale / surrender / retreat:**  
The roots stop moving when the characters withdraw or speak a sincere oath not to despoil the tomb. Root-Scrabblers retreat at half their number or when the living outer roots are protected.

## Terrain and Environment

- The entrance rubble is uneven ground; a creature moving hurriedly must succeed on the root-navigation check or stumble.
- The upright stone provides half cover to a creature crouching behind it, but it is too heavy to move during the adventure.
- Light is absent unless supplied by the characters or the faint gold pulse from deeper inside.
- The narrow connection to the central junction is single-file for a short stretch; a creature cannot move through an occupied square without the normal movement rules and a willing ally moving aside.
- Loud impacts echo into every branch and count as a plausible reason to advance the Tomb Pulse.

## Hazards

The Root-Mouth hazard is detailed in Appendix A. It is a physical obstacle with a fail-forward consequence, not a hidden instant-kill trap.

## Clues and Information

| Clue | How Found | What It Reveals | If Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snapped brass key-ring loop | Search the dust or follow the scrape marks | Dagna reached the tomb and was forced farther inside | Dagna calls from the south branch later |
| Heavy boot trail | Investigation or Survival | Sella moved a dense object from the north branch toward the central junction | Sella mentions the amber shard if confronted |
| Handprint and oath-marker | Clean or interpret the upright stone | The tomb's magic is tied to spoken promises | The east ledger repeats the principle |
| Grey grit in the roots | Nature, Religion, or observation | The corruption is coming from inside the tomb, not merely from the forest | The grey water becomes unmistakable at Pulse 1 |

## Treasure / Resources

The spring-keepers' pry bar and bitterroot bundles remain useful. A careful character can collect one additional bitterroot bundle from a living root mat with a DC 12 Wisdom (Nature) check. It has no sale value but works like the supplied bundles.

## Development

The party reaches the central junction. If they entered quietly, they hear a metal scrape from the north branch and a muffled voice from the south. If they caused a loud collapse, Sella has time to move the shard from the north reliquary to the central socket, and the next scene begins at Tomb Pulse 1.

## Failure Forward

If the characters fail to open the entrance safely, they still enter, but one character starts with 1d6 damage and the Tomb Pulse advances. If they retreat, the grey water reaches the outer shelf before they return; the adventure remains possible, but Brackenford's spring will take longer to cleanse after success.

## Scaling

For a weaker group, remove the Root-Scrabblers entirely and let the first root check succeed after a visible cost such as damaged equipment. For a stronger group, use two Root-Scrabblers only after a loud approach and let one cling to the upright marker, forcing the characters to choose between fighting and preserving the clue.

## Transition

From the central junction, the party can take the narrow north corridor to the round reliquary, the east connection to the records room, or the south corridor toward Dagna's voice. The tomb does not force an order. Sella is most likely to be in the north chamber if the characters arrive quickly.

# 11. PART 2 — THE BROKEN ACCOUNTING

## Purpose

Part 2 turns the map's branching plan into an investigation. The north chamber presents the tangible relic and Sella; the east room provides the history and procedure. Together they reveal what must be done in the central junction. Allow 60–75 minutes, less if the characters make a quick deal with Sella.

## Situation

The central chamber is a crossroad of dark stone. Hairline amber lines run through the floor toward each branch, but the line to the north is dim and the line to the south pulses like a weak heartbeat. In the round north reliquary, the amber shard is either in Sella's hand or resting crookedly against the edge of the socket. The east records room is intact enough to search, though the cabinet or chest has a dwarven accounting seal.

## Scene Introduction

> The central chamber is not a hall so much as a knot pulled tight: four passages meet around a floor of fitted stone, and every seam points toward the same unseen centre. To the north, warm light leaks from a round room. To the east, a smaller chamber waits behind a narrow approach, its stone chest untouched. From the south comes one muffled blow from inside the ground.

## Player Objective

Learn the shard's purpose, obtain the names and terms of the oath, and decide what to do with Sella before the Tomb Pulse worsens.

## Key Features

- **Central socket:** A shallow octagonal recess in the central chamber's floor, connected by amber lines to the branches. The shard can be placed here, but it will not stabilize without the names and correct procedure.
- **North round reliquary:** A circular stone room with a low ringed dais. The amber shard gives off gold light here; its mounting is visibly damaged.
- **East records room:** A small rectangular chamber containing the visible stone chest/cabinet. Its shelves hold thin metal leaves, name tablets, and a maintenance diagram.
- **Echoing junction:** A voice spoken in one branch can be heard in the others. The Hollow Oath uses this to repeat a character's last dishonest claim.

## Approaches

### Combat

If the characters confront Sella and she refuses to bargain, she fights alongside two Oathbound Skeletons if the Tomb Pulse is 1 or higher. If Pulse 0, she has only herself and uses the reliquary's ring for cover. Sella's goal is to escape, not kill; when reduced to 10 hit points or fewer, she drops or offers the shard and asks for a route out.

### Social

Sella is willing to bargain from the moment she is seen. She gives up the shard for safe passage, a promise that she can keep one ordinary funerary token, or proof that the shard is not valuable to collectors. Showing her the records grants advantage on the next check to persuade her. If the party lies about the shard, the Hollow Oath repeats the lie and Sella becomes harder to influence.

### Skill / Problem Solving

The north reliquary can be searched for a safe way to handle the shard, while the east room can be opened by matching a name tablet to the handprint mark from Part 1. There are several valid methods: identify the matching symbols, use dwarven tools, force the stone chest, or ask Sella for the copied phrase. The records are not protected by a single binary lock.

### Stealth / Avoidance

The party can bypass Sella, search the east room, and return to the junction. If they do, Sella follows at a distance and appears during the finale with the shard, unless a character leaves a credible escape route for her. A quiet party can remove the shard from the reliquary without a fight, but must still resolve the engine later.

### Creative Solutions

A character can use the amber light to project the shard's inscription, cast a divination, or compare the records to the oath-marker. Magic that detects thoughts hears many overlapping names but not a complete sentence. A character who makes the chamber safer rather than merely opening the chest may grant one automatic success on the finale's restoration track.

## Checks and Challenges

| Task | Skill / Method | Difficulty | Success | Failure / Complication |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Handle the amber shard safely | DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana, History, or Investigation) or Wisdom (Religion) | 13 | Learn it regulates the engine and must return to the central socket | The holder takes 1d6 psychic damage and hears the word “account” repeated; the clue remains available |
| Open the records chest | DC 13 Dexterity with thieves' tools, Strength, or Intelligence (Investigation) | 13 | The chest opens without damaging the names | A metal leaf bends; one name is obscured until repaired or supplied by Dagna |
| Interpret the maintenance diagram | DC 14 Intelligence (History, Investigation, or Arcana) | 14 | Learn that three names and three actions are needed to restore the engine | The diagram is understood only as “return, speak, bind,” imposing disadvantage on the first restoration check |
| Persuade Sella to cooperate | DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion, Deception, or Intimidation) | 14 | Sella gives the shard or agrees to distract the spirit later | She retreats to the central junction and the Tomb Pulse advances if the exchange becomes loud |
| Reconstruct a missing name | DC 13 Intelligence (History), Wisdom (Religion), or comparison of tablets | 13 | Recover the name from partial letters and a burial mark | Dagna can provide it in Part 3; the party loses one possible automatic finale success |

## Creatures / NPCs

- Sella Varn, using the Appendix A stat block.
- Two Oathbound Skeletons, only if a fight begins at Pulse 1 or later; use the Appendix A stat block.
- The Hollow Oath is present only as whispers and reflections here, not as a full combatant.

## Tactics / Behaviour

**Before conflict:**  
Sella keeps the amber shard wrapped in cloth and stands where she can reach either the north corridor or central socket. The skeletons remain still until she calls them or the shard is threatened.

**During conflict:**  
Sella uses the narrow corridor to break line of sight, throws a dust pouch to obscure a square, and tries to reach the central junction. The skeletons block pursuit rather than chase wounded characters. If a character speaks a recorded name aloud, one skeleton loses its next reaction as it hesitates.

**Morale / surrender / retreat:**  
Sella surrenders at 10 hit points or fewer, when both skeletons fall, or when the party offers safe passage. The skeletons collapse when the shard is returned to its north mounting or when Sella yields it. If Sella escapes, she remains available as a frightened witness in the finale.

## Terrain and Environment

- The north reliquary's ringed dais gives half cover but is difficult terrain for a creature climbing over it.
- The central junction is open enough for four characters to spread out, but the narrow branch corridors make retreat single-file.
- The east records room has shelves and the stone chest; shelves can be toppled with a DC 12 Strength check, creating difficult terrain in a 5-foot square.
- The amber shard sheds dim light in a 10-foot radius while exposed.
- At Pulse 2, two 5-foot squares adjacent to the central socket become root-choked difficult terrain.

## Hazards

The amber shard deals psychic backlash only when mishandled or used in an incorrect socket. It is not an attackable creature and cannot be casually destroyed by an ordinary weapon without invoking the finale's destruction procedure.

## Clues and Information

| Clue | How Found | What It Reveals | If Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empty north mounting | Search the round chamber | The shard was removed, not consumed | Sella admits she took it |
| Copied phrase in Sella's satchel | Search or persuade Sella | “Return the light; speak the owed names; bind the living word.” | The east diagram gives the same sequence |
| Three name tablets | Open and search the east chest | Restoration and bargaining both require specific names | Dagna knows the names from memory |
| Vhaldrik's sealed admission | Read the lowest metal leaf | Vhaldrik ordered the tunnel sealed after the workers surrendered | The Hollow Oath states it during the finale |

## Treasure / Resources

The records chest contains the three name tablets, a dwarven silver stylus worth 25 gp, a small brass seal worth 15 gp, and two potions of healing. The amber shard is a quest relic, not ordinary treasure. Sella carries 18 gp, thieves' tools, a dust pouch, and a nonmagical iron memorial nail taken from another tomb.

## Development

If the characters recover the shard and records, mark one **Engine Advantage**: the first finale objective check automatically succeeds if the characters describe using the correct sequence. If they recruit Sella, she can use her action during the finale to distract the Hollow Oath once, imposing disadvantage on its next Oathlash. If they threaten or humiliate her, she withholds the copied phrase and tries to flee.

## Failure Forward

If the party cannot open the chest, the stone chest cracks during a later tremor and the tablets spill into the central junction. If they lose the shard, Sella or the Hollow Oath leaves a visible amber trail to it. If the party fights and Sella escapes, her satchel remains behind unless she has time to retrieve it. No failed check removes all access to the names.

## Scaling

For a weaker group, have Sella flee immediately after one round and use no skeletons. For a stronger group, let Sella begin with cover and have one skeleton use the Help action each round rather than adding more creatures. Do not add a third skeleton if the south crypt will also be fought.

## Transition

The characters can descend south immediately, return to the north reliquary, or use the records to prepare the central socket. Dagna's voice becomes clearer if they approach the south corridor. If the party spends about 30 minutes here, advance the Tomb Pulse and describe the round reliquary's gold light flickering.

# 12. PART 3 — THE NAMES BELOW

## Purpose

Part 3 offers rescue, moral context, and a meaningful preparation choice. It also gives the party a chance to reduce the finale's difficulty by freeing Dagna and recovering the complete names. Run it in 45–60 minutes, or shorten it to a tense rescue if the session is running late.

## Situation

The south corridor opens into the tomb's largest visible room. A long stone slab occupies its centre, surrounded by several round burial lids set into the floor. Dagna is pinned behind the slab by a fallen section of stone and roots. At Pulse 0 or 1 the lids are still; at Pulse 2, two lids have shifted and the Oathbound Skeletons inside are active. The room is not a maze: the danger comes from crossing it while deciding which stone to move.

## Scene Introduction

> The southern room is wider than the passages allow, a communal chamber built around a long stone slab. Round lids punctuate the floor like dark coins, each marked with a different tool or trade. Beyond the slab, a brass key-ring glints in a hand pressed against the stone. “Do not lift the wrong dead,” Dagna says. “The ones who were betrayed are not the ones who should bear the blame.”

## Player Objective

Reach Dagna, free her without needlessly disturbing the crypt, and obtain the names and practical instruction needed for the finale.

## Key Features

- **Long central slab:** A funerary worktable and maintenance cover. Its underside hides a brass lever connected to the central engine. Moving or bracing it can make the finale safer.
- **Round burial lids:** Several marked lids represent miners, masons, and keepers. Two have shifted at Pulse 2; others remain sealed unless deliberately disturbed.
- **Dagna's position:** She is trapped on the far side of the long slab, conscious and able to speak, but cannot pull herself free while the roots tighten.
- **South chamber floor:** Open flagstone around the slab and lids; a creature can circle the room, though the lids create hazardous footing.

## Approaches

### Combat

At Pulse 2 or later, two Oathbound Skeletons emerge from the shifted lids. They fight to keep characters away from Dagna and the maintenance lever. They do not open the other lids. At Pulse 0 or 1, combat is avoidable unless the characters deliberately break a lid or attack the roots.

### Social

Dagna can guide the party from behind the slab. She tells them which marked lids are safe to brace and which are tied to the engine. She admits that she hid Vhaldrik's full confession and asks the characters to read it before deciding whether to restore his system. A character who promises to preserve the workers' names gains Dagna's complete cooperation.

### Skill / Problem Solving

Freeing Dagna is a small group challenge: two successes before two failures using DC 13 Strength (Athletics), Dexterity with tools, Intelligence (Investigation), Wisdom (Medicine or Religion), or suitable magic. Each character can describe a different contribution. A success loosens roots or shifts the slab; a failure causes 1d6 bludgeoning damage to the acting character as stone slips, but the attempt remains possible.

The brass lever under the slab requires one additional DC 13 check to locate and pull. If pulled, it does not solve the crisis; it makes the central socket's surrounding floor stable, granting advantage on one finale objective check and preventing the first forced movement from the Hollow Oath.

### Stealth / Avoidance

At Pulse 0 or 1, characters can circle the slab and reach Dagna without touching a lid. At Pulse 2, the skeletons are already awake, but a quiet party can avoid them by moving along the wall and speaking Dagna's name. This does not remove the enemies, but it allows the party to free Dagna before combat begins.

### Creative Solutions

A character can use rope, pitons, summoned creatures, or a wedge from the west antechamber to stabilize the slab. Healing magic does not remove the stone, but it can keep Dagna conscious. If the characters use the silver stylus from the east records room to copy names onto the lids, the skeletons hesitate and the party gains advantage on one social or engine check.

## Checks and Challenges

| Task | Skill / Method | Difficulty | Success | Failure / Complication |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Free Dagna | DC 13 Athletics, tools, Investigation, Medicine, Religion, or suitable magic | 13 | One success toward two; on the second, Dagna is free | Acting character takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage and the roots tighten; after two total failures, advance Pulse by 1 |
| Identify safe lids | DC 12 History, Religion, or inspect the trade marks | 12 | Characters avoid disturbing the wrong burial | A lid shifts; if Pulse is 2 or higher, one skeleton gains surprise only if the group is careless |
| Find the maintenance lever | DC 13 Investigation, Perception, or knowledge of dwarven tombs | 13 | Lever is found and can grant finale advantage | It is found only after a tremor; the finale begins with the first floor square flooded |
| Calm an awakened skeleton | DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion), Wisdom (Religion), or speak its name from the ledger | 13 | It lowers its weapon for one round or withdraws into its lid | It attacks the nearest creature, but the name remains known for a later attempt |

## Creatures / NPCs

- Keeper Dagna Flintvein.
- Two Oathbound Skeletons at Pulse 2 or 3, or if the party deliberately disturbs the crypt.
- The other buried dead remain inactive unless the characters choose to desecrate the lids; do not add more combatants.

## Tactics / Behaviour

**Before conflict:**  
The skeletons rise between the party and Dagna, using the long slab as cover. They do not pursue characters into the corridor if the party retreats.

**During conflict:**  
One skeleton blocks access to Dagna while the other attacks the character interacting with the slab or lever. If a character speaks a recorded name, the addressed skeleton must choose between attacking and protecting its lid; it loses its reaction that round.

**Morale / surrender / retreat:**  
The skeletons fall inert when Dagna is free and the maintenance lever is pulled, when both are reduced to half hit points, or when the party completes a sincere naming ritual. They are not intelligent negotiators, but they respond to proper funerary conduct.

## Terrain and Environment

- The long slab grants half cover and divides the room; moving around its ends costs normal movement, but climbing over it is difficult terrain.
- Each round lid is a 5-foot area of uneven stone. A creature forced onto one must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity saving throw or fall prone; this is not a hidden trap.
- The south chamber is the best place to take a short rest, but doing so advances the Tomb Pulse by 1 and may awaken the skeletons.
- The brass lever, once pulled, emits a low tone that can be heard in every branch.

## Hazards

Disturbing an unopened lid without a clear reason causes roots to lash out. The character must make a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw, taking 2d6 bludgeoning damage on a failure or half as much on a success. The lid then reseals; the dead inside do not emerge unless the GM wants a dramatic consequence for deliberate desecration.

## Clues and Information

| Clue | How Found | What It Reveals | If Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dagna's testimony | Speak with her or free her | The amber shard regulates; the names make the oath truthful; the lever stabilizes the floor | The east records and the Hollow Oath repeat these facts |
| Trade-marked lids | Read the marks or compare to tablets | The south crypt contains the workers' memorial, not Vhaldrik's personal guard | The party can still avoid the lids by staying near the wall |
| Brass maintenance lever | Search beneath the long slab | The engine can be stabilized without destroying it | Restoration remains possible but the first finale check is harder |
| Dagna's hidden confession | Ask why she withheld the record | The keeper fears the living will use the dead as a political weapon | The moral choice remains clear from the east-room confession |

## Treasure / Resources

Dagna gives the characters the **Keeper's Brass Key**, which grants advantage on one check made to manipulate the central socket or a tomb mechanism. She also gives them a small pouch containing 30 gp in old dwarven trade coins, intended for memorial offerings, and insists that the coins be returned if they choose to bargain with the Hollow Oath.

## Development

If Dagna is freed, she can accompany the party to the central junction but will not enter the shard's immediate glow. Once during the finale, she can use her action to grant advantage on an objective check, explain a historical detail, or hold back a root surge so one character can move safely. If she remains in the crypt, she continues speaking the names, granting the same benefit but only once.

## Failure Forward

If Dagna cannot be freed, she tells the characters the names through the roots and remains alive until the finale ends. If she is killed, her brass key and testimony are found beneath the slab. If the skeletons overwhelm the party, they drag Dagna back behind the slab rather than pursue beyond the room; the characters can regroup in the central junction and return after taking a cost.

## Scaling

For a weaker group, begin with Pulse 1 regardless of the clock and let the skeletons collapse when Dagna is freed. For a stronger group, add a root surge at initiative count 20 in this room: one 5-foot square becomes difficult terrain, but do not add more undead.

## Transition

Dagna urges the characters to resolve the engine now. Returning to the central junction with the shard, tablets, key, and testimony triggers the final manifestation. If they have not visited the east room, Dagna directs them there first unless the Tomb Pulse is already 3.

# 13. OPTIONAL ENCOUNTER — THE UNQUIET LEDGER

## Trigger

The characters trigger this scene if they search the east records room a second time, read the names aloud, or return to the room after speaking with Dagna. It can also replace the south-crypt combat for a group that prefers a quieter adventure.

## Purpose

This scene turns the records from exposition into an active relationship with the dead. It rewards curiosity, clarifies the bargain route, and gives the characters a concrete way to prepare for the finale.

## Encounter

The three metal name tablets warm when placed together. Charcoal letters appear in the ledger's empty margins, completing the names of the workers and adding one sentence in dwarven: “We did not ask to be avenged; we asked to be remembered before the gate was closed.” Each character may contribute one sentence describing what they believe should happen to a promise that was broken. The GM should accept sincere statements without a check.

If a character lies, grandstands, or claims that the dead deserved their fate, the ledger snaps shut and the character takes 1d6 psychic damage. If the party speaks sincerely, each character who participated gains advantage on their first Charisma, History, Religion, or Arcana check in the finale. This benefit does not stack with itself, but it can apply to different characters.

The ledger also shows a charcoal diagram of the central socket and the phrase “three voices, one living word.” This is a backup for any missing engine clue.

## Reward / Consequence

The party receives the **Ledger's Witness** benefit described above and learns that the Hollow Oath wants acknowledgement rather than indiscriminate killing. If they close the ledger without reading the names, nothing attacks them, but the benefit is lost. This optional encounter should take 10–15 minutes.

# 14. FINALE — THE OATH AT THE JUNCTION

## Situation

When the characters bring the shard, the records, Dagna's testimony, or even only their understanding of the breach to the central junction, the amber lines flare. Roots push through the seams, grey water spreads from the south corridor, and the Hollow Oath forms above the central socket as a silhouette made of dust, pale light, and overlapping faces. Sella appears if she escaped, either to return the shard or to seek cover.

The finale is a hybrid encounter. Defeating the manifestation buys time but does not by itself seal the tomb. The characters must complete one of three objective tracks while surviving the Hollow Oath and environmental pressure.

## Immediate Stakes

At Tomb Pulse 3, the manifestation begins forcing grey water through the west connection toward the root-mouth. If the party spends three consecutive rounds without making progress on a chosen objective, advance the scene's danger: a new 5-foot square becomes difficult terrain and the Hollow Oath uses Oathlash against one target. After the third such escalation, the spring is contaminated even if the tomb is later sealed; this is partial success at best.

## Scene Introduction

> The four passages answer one another with a single sound. Gold light races from the north, grey water spills from the south, and the stone beneath your feet opens into a darkness that is not a hole. Faces surface in it—miners, masons, strangers whose names are worn away. A voice speaks from all of them at once: “You have come for the light. Have you come for the word?”

## Final Objective

Choose, or discover through play, one of the following:

- **Restore:** Return the amber shard to the central socket and speak three owed names, completing three successes before two failures.
- **Destroy:** Break the shard and disable the oath-engine through three successful attacks or destructive checks, accepting the backlash.
- **Bargain:** Name the betrayal, acknowledge the harm, and promise a credible memorial or restitution through three sincere successes before two failures.

The characters may change routes during the scene, but a failed route leaves visible damage or consumes an action; the GM should not reset the track without a story reason.

## Available Approaches

### Direct Confrontation

The Hollow Oath can be attacked. Use the Appendix A manifestation. At 0 hit points it becomes silent and cannot use Oathlash, but the chosen objective still must be completed. The characters may also attack roots, move the shard, or defend an ally; treat those as normal actions with clear fictional effects rather than requiring every creative action to fit the three tracks.

### Social / Negotiated Resolution

The Hollow Oath is willing to listen if characters speak truthfully. Use the Bargain track below. Sella's testimony, the east ledger, Dagna's confession, and the Ledger's Witness benefit can turn a check into an automatic success when the characters supply a specific, sincere statement.

### Skill / Environmental Resolution

Use the Restore or Destroy tracks. A character adjacent to the socket can use an action for a DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana, History, or Investigation), Wisdom (Religion), Strength (Athletics), or another justified check. A character can Help normally, but a single character should not repeat the same method without explaining what has changed.

### Alternate Resolution Enabled by Earlier Choices

- If the party has the **Engine Advantage**, the first Restore check automatically succeeds.
- If Dagna is free, she can grant advantage once, as described in Part 3.
- If Sella cooperates, she can use her action once to distract the manifestation; its next Oathlash is made with disadvantage, and the next objective check gains advantage.
- If the Ledger's Witness benefit was earned, sincere characters can make one Bargain check automatically successful.
- If the party pulled the south-room lever, the first creature moved by the Hollow Oath is not pushed and the floor around the socket remains stable.

## Battlefield / Scene Features

- **Central socket:** The objective location, adjacent to the floor's amber lines. A creature within 5 feet can attempt an objective action.
- **North corridor and reliquary:** Gold light and the shard's former mounting. A character can retreat here for cover, but the corridor is narrow.
- **East records connection:** The ledger and tablets can be consulted as an action, granting advantage on a relevant check if a character has not already used that source.
- **South corridor and crypt approach:** Grey water enters here at Pulse 2; the first 10 feet are difficult terrain at Pulse 3.
- **West antechamber connection:** The root-mouth lies beyond it. If the party reaches the west chamber with the shard, the Hollow Oath attempts to follow the root network.
- **Root lines:** At Pulse 2, two 5-foot squares chosen by the GM are difficult terrain. At Pulse 3, a creature ending its turn on grey water makes a DC 14 Constitution saving throw, taking 2d6 poison damage on a failure or half as much on a success.

## Opponent Goals

The Hollow Oath wants its truth acknowledged and wants the shard prevented from becoming a tool for a living thief. It attacks to stop false promises, not merely to reduce hit points. If the characters show that they will preserve the names and make restitution, it becomes less aggressive. If they try to flee with the shard, it pursues to the root-mouth.

## Tactics and Escalation

### Opening

The Hollow Oath manifests and uses Oathlash against the character holding the shard or making the first false claim. It asks one question: “Who closed the gate?” It does not demand an immediate answer, but a dismissive response counts as a failed Bargain attempt if that route is being used.

### Escalation

At initiative count 20, the manifestation uses one environmental effect: Oathlash, root surge, or Grey Flood if Pulse 3. If the characters have fought the south skeletons and Pulse is 2 or higher, two Oathbound Skeletons can rise from the central floor once; they collapse when the objective reaches two successes. Do not add them if the party is already badly depleted.

Every two failures on an objective track advances the Tomb Pulse by 1, to a maximum of 3. Each failure also causes the acting character to take 1d6 psychic damage on Restore or Bargain, or 2d6 force damage on Destroy with a DC 14 Dexterity save for half.

### Breaking Point

At half hit points, the Hollow Oath stops attacking for a moment and says, “The stone can break. The word cannot.” This gives the characters a clear invitation to bargain. If the party completes three Restore or Bargain successes, it releases its hold even if still above 0 hit points. If reduced to 0 hit points, it becomes quiet but the engine remains unresolved.

## Objective Tracks

### Restore the Engine

The party must possess the amber shard and know at least three names. A character adjacent to the socket uses an action to make a DC 14 Arcana, History, Investigation, Religion, or similarly justified check while speaking a different name. Three successes before two failures stabilize the engine. If the party has no names, Dagna or the ledger supplies them; the first attempt is made with disadvantage.

On success, the shard locks into place, the amber lines become steady, and the Hollow Oath disperses into a quiet chorus. The engine remains active and the tomb can be sealed again. On failure, the shard cracks; the party may still use the Destroy track, but Brackenford's spring is tainted for a week.

### Destroy the Engine

A character adjacent to the socket uses an action to make a weapon or spell attack against AC 15, or a DC 14 Strength (Athletics), Arcana, or other clearly destructive check. Three successes destroy the shard and disable the engine. Each successful attempt deals 2d6 force damage to the acting character, with a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw for half, and causes the Hollow Oath to use Oathlash immediately if it can.

On the third success, each creature within 10 feet of the socket makes a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw, taking 3d6 force damage on a failure or half as much on a success. The tomb shudders, but the roots eventually recede. The dead are not released; the engine and the shard are lost.

### Bargain with the Hollow Oath

The party must complete three distinct acts: name the betrayal; acknowledge the harm without excusing it; and promise a credible memorial or restitution. A character within 10 feet uses an action and makes a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion), Wisdom (Religion), Intelligence (History), or another justified check. A sincere statement supported by the records, Dagna, or the Ledger's Witness can succeed automatically.

Three successes before two failures cause the Hollow Oath to release the tomb. The shard becomes inert but whole, and the characters must carry the names back to Brackenford. The spring is safe once the memorial is raised. A failure does not make the route impossible, but a dishonest or evasive statement causes 1d6 psychic damage and advances the Pulse after the second failed attempt.

## Victory Conditions

The characters achieve full success if:

- They complete Restore, Destroy, or Bargain before the Hollow Oath escapes through the west root-mouth.
- They preserve or recover the workers' names, either in the east records or through Dagna's testimony.

Partial success occurs if:

- The engine is destroyed but the names are lost; Brackenford is saved, but the Hollow Oath is remembered only as an unnamed haunting.
- The party seals the breach after Pulse 3; the spring is contaminated for a week and the tomb suffers major damage.
- The party bargains but cannot promise a memorial to the hamlet; the Hollow Oath remains bound to the cliff as a demanding guardian.

Failure occurs if:

- The Hollow Oath reaches the west root-mouth with the shard or the party abandons the tomb after Pulse 3.
- The party completes none of the three tracks before three escalation events occur after the manifestation.

## Development

When the objective resolves, the central chamber becomes quiet. Restore leaves a steady amber glow in the north reliquary and makes the tomb a safe memorial. Destroy causes a controlled collapse of the central socket but leaves the four mapped rooms accessible for one final search. Bargain turns the roots white and still; the Hollow Oath remains as a voice that answers only when the names are spoken respectfully.

Give the players a short scene to choose what to carry home: the records, the inert shard, Sella's confession, Dagna's key, or a memorial nail. Then proceed to the conclusion.

# 15. CONCLUSION

## Full Success

The grey water clears after the characters complete the restoration, destruction, or bargain route. If they restored the engine, Dagna relights the north reliquary and seals the root-mouth with a proper funerary mark. If they destroyed it, the tomb remains a quiet ruin but the names are preserved. If they bargained, the roots become pale and the Hollow Oath keeps watch over the dead, answering only to respectful remembrance.

Brackenford receives safe water after the spring-keepers flush the source and burn the first bitterroot bundle. The characters can decide whether the public account names Vhaldrik's crime, credits Dagna's stewardship, or leaves the dwarves to make that decision themselves.

## Partial Success

If the characters save Brackenford but lose the names, the spring is safe but the dead have no public memorial. One night each year, a voice repeats “three doors, one promise” from the cliff. If the party seals the tomb after Pulse 3, the hamlet survives but the cliff is scarred, the tomb's branches partially collapse, and the characters cannot recover every record. If they bargain without securing a memorial, the Hollow Oath remains a difficult local guardian that may call on them later.

## Failure

If the Hollow Oath escapes, grey roots reach the spring and the dead of any newly disturbed grave in the valley can hear its accusation. Brackenford evacuates uphill. Dagna, if alive, becomes the keeper of a spreading memorial road; Sella disappears with her guilt and may later return seeking help. The adventure still leaves a clear future task: recover the shard, gather the names, and make the promise the party avoided.

## NPC and Faction Reactions

- **Dagna Flintvein:** Celebrates truthful restoration, accepts destruction if the names survive, and refuses to call a bargain a victory until the memorial is built.
- **Sella Varn:** If spared, returns the iron memorial nail and gives up grave robbing for a time. If exposed publicly, she repays the hamlet through labour rather than imprisonment if the spring-keepers permit it.
- **Brackenford Spring-Keepers:** Pay the promised 150 gp for any outcome that saves the spring; for full success they add 50 gp in supplies and name the characters honorary water-wardens.
- **The Hollow Oath:** Disperses after restoration or destruction, or remains as a watchful, demanding voice after a bargain.

## Consequences of Earlier Choices

- Freeing Dagna provides a finale advantage and preserves a living witness.
- Treating Sella fairly can remove one finale hazard and adds her confession to the historical record.
- Reading the ledger's names strengthens the Bargain route.
- Advancing the Tomb Pulse makes the finale more dangerous but does not remove any route.
- Preserving the living roots prevents a cliff collapse and leaves the tomb accessible as a memorial.
- Taking the amber shard as treasure without resolving the engine triggers the escape outcome unless the characters return and complete a route.

## Rewards

- **Experience / advancement:** Award normal advancement for a completed one-shot under the group's 2024 progression method; the adventure contains enough danger and discovery for the party's next milestone.
- **Treasure:** 150 gp from Brackenford, plus 30 gp in old dwarven coins if Dagna is rescued and the party returns them to the memorial or accepts them honestly.
- **Reputation / favour:** Brackenford offers free lodging and supplies for future travel; dwarven communities that learn the truth recognize the party as keepers of the Stone-Sworn names.
- **Narrative reward:** The party's chosen memorial becomes a safe place where lost travellers can leave messages for the dead.
- **Special item / knowledge:** The Keeper's Brass Key and, depending on the route, an inert amber shard or access to the restored tomb.

## Future Hooks

- A second cliff in the mountain has begun growing the same pale roots, but its tomb has no records.
- A dwarven claimant wants to suppress Vhaldrik's confession and offers a reward for the east-room ledger.
- The Hollow Oath's final whispered name does not belong to any worker listed in the tomb.

## Hollow Oath Manifestation

*Medium Undead Spirit, unaligned; custom manifestation*

**Armor Class** 15  
**Hit Points** 70 (custom environmental boss)  
**Speed** 0 ft., fly 30 ft. (hover)

**STR** 8 (-1) **DEX** 14 (+2) **CON** 14 (+2) **INT** 12 (+1) **WIS** 16 (+3) **CHA** 16 (+3)

**Damage Resistances** necrotic, psychic

### Traits

**Root-Bound.** The manifestation cannot willingly move beyond the west root-mouth while the amber shard remains inside the tomb. It can pass through a creature's space but cannot end its movement there.

**Many Voices.** The manifestation has advantage on checks to identify a lie spoken within 30 feet, but a sincere statement supported by the records can grant an automatic success on the Bargain track as described in the finale.

**Unresolved Oath.** Reducing the manifestation to 0 Hit Points silences it but does not complete the engine objective.

### Actions

**Oathlash.** One creature within 30 feet makes a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw. On a failed save, the creature takes 9 (2d8) psychic damage and is pushed up to 10 feet. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage and is not pushed.

**Whispered Accusation (Recharge 5–6).** One creature within 30 feet that has spoken a lie or knowingly withheld a relevant truth makes a DC 14 Charisma saving throw. On a failure, it takes 7 (2d6) psychic damage and cannot take Reactions until the start of its next turn. On a success, it takes half as much damage and suffers no other effect. If no creature has lied or knowingly withheld a relevant truth, the manifestation cannot use this action and uses Oathlash instead.

### Initiative 20 Environmental Effect

The manifestation uses one effect; it cannot use the same effect in two consecutive rounds.

- **Root Surge:** One 5-foot square within 30 feet becomes difficult terrain until the next initiative count 20.
- **Oathlash:** Use Oathlash against one creature, but the target has advantage on its saving throw if it has not lied or knowingly withheld a relevant truth.
- **Grey Flood:** Only at Pulse 3. Each creature standing in grey water makes a DC 14 Constitution saving throw, taking 7 (2d6) poison damage on a failure or half as much on a success.

## Scaling Encounter D

For a weaker party, reduce the manifestation to 55 Hit Points, lower objective DCs to 13, and omit Grey Flood damage at Pulse 3. For a stronger party, increase it to 85 Hit Points or allow Oathlash to target two different creatures at Pulse 3. Do not add more than two skeletons, and do not combine the stronger version with every optional combat.

## Special Items

### Amber Oath-Shard

*Wondrous item, quest relic, not normally transferable as treasure*

The shard is a palm-sized piece of amber heartwood sealed in dwarven metal. While exposed, it sheds dim golden light in a 10-foot radius. It is warm to the touch and cannot be safely used as a generic spell component or sold as an ordinary gemstone.

In the tomb, the shard is the regulator for the oath-engine. ## Fast Tomb Pulse Procedure

When the clock advances, describe one sensory change before applying mechanics: a root tightening, a gold light flickering, a lid knocking, or grey water spreading.
